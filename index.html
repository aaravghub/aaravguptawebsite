Engineering Impact

FTC

Lead Mechanical Designer — Team 30439

As Lead Mechanical Designer, I was responsible for the full mechanical architecture of the robot, from drivetrain layout to intake geometry and end-effector packaging. Every mechanism was designed in SolidWorks with strict tolerance control and real-world assembly constraints in mind.

I led iterative design cycles where parts were prototyped, tested, failed, and redesigned under competition timelines. This included optimizing center of mass, reducing friction losses, and balancing structural rigidity with weight limits imposed by FTC regulations.

Beyond CAD, I worked closely with software to ensure autonomous reliability. Mechanical decisions were made to simplify odometry, reduce backlash, and improve repeatability across matches. The result was a robot designed not just to score, but to survive an entire season of abuse.

Mentoring

73 Students Across 5 Teams

Mentored over 73 students across 5 FTC and FLL teams, providing technical guidance in CAD, mechanical design, drivetrain theory, and competition strategy. My role extended beyond answering questions — I helped teams develop engineering intuition.

I taught students how to think in systems: identifying failure points, designing for repair, and making tradeoffs between speed, strength, and reliability. This included live design reviews, manufacturing walkthroughs, and post-match failure analysis.

Many of these teams progressed from basic kits to fully custom mechanisms, improved autonomous consistency, and stronger competition performance. The goal was never just winning — it was building engineers who could reason through problems independently.

Every mechanism is designed in CAD, stress-tested virtually, then iterated physically. Failure modes are intentional — I design systems expecting abuse.

Started 3D printing at age 7, initially producing small personal projects and learning the fundamentals of slicing, materials, and printer calibration. Over time, this evolved into a structured manufacturing operation.

Revenue was fully reinvested into higher-end equipment, including additional printers and a laser cutter. This operation wasn't a hobby — it functioned as a real manufacturing pipeline with cost tracking, throughput optimization, and continuous improvement.

Tabla

Visharat Level Training

Currently pursuing the Visharat level in Tabla, an advanced degree in Hindustani classical music. This requires an in-depth understanding of complex mathematical compositions, including Tihais, Kayadas, and Relas, executed across 16-beat cycles (Teental) and irregular time signatures.

The training emphasizes extreme temporal precision, independent limb coordination, and the ability to sustain rapid permutations of strokes at high BPMs. It is a study of pattern recognition and recursive structures in an auditory medium.
